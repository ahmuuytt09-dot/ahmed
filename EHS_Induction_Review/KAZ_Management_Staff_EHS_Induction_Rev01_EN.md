### SLIDE 16 — Electrical safety rules (1)
1. Treat every electrical circuit as **live until proven dead**
2. Isolate the source before work; **LOTO** to prevent re-energisation
3. **Prove the tester before and after testing** — then prove dead
4. Only an **Authorized Electrical Person (AEP)** may isolate, de-isolate or energise; each worker applies a **personal lock**
5. Never work on live equipment unless formally authorised and only under an **Electrical Hot/Commissioning Permit**
6. Use insulated tools suitable for the voltage; verify calibration and condition
7. Maintain safe approach distances at all times (Slide 31–32)
8. Keep panels and access routes clear; report damaged equipment, cables or covers immediately
**Golden Rule: ISOLATE — LOCK OUT — TAG OUT — TEST BEFORE TOUCH**

### SLIDE 17 — Electrical safety rules (2) — Arc flash and switching
- Arc flash risk assessment exists for this site: respect the **Arc Flash Boundary**, use the specified PPE category, never bypass barriers or interlocks
- Only competent, appointed persons may switch, ground or energise (Operations Manager, HV Safety Supervisor, Switching Supervisor, AEP)
- Work within **30 m of a high-voltage system** requires agreement with the plant owner and physical demarcation
- **Stop all work during thunderstorms** — cranes, lifts, scaffolding and installation work must be suspended (lightning and induced voltage)
- Report any contact with a conductor, any flash and any damaged insulation immediately — do not touch the casualty before isolation

### SLIDE 21 — Risk management: RA, SJA and LMRA

- **LMRA (Last Minute Risk Assessment):** short check before every task — What can hurt me? What has changed? Are controls in place? If in doubt — stop and ask
- **Management of Change:** if the method, equipment, personnel or site conditions change, work stops and the assessment is revised and approved

### SLIDE 25 — LOTO and energy isolation
- Cover **all** energy sources: electrical, mechanical, pneumatic, hydraulic, thermal, stored energy
- Sequence: notify → isolate → lock & tag → release stored energy → verify **zero energy** → work
- Every worker applies their **own personal lock**; keys are kept in the lockbox under the AEP’s control
- **PTW validity ≠ LOTO validity** — expiry of a permit does not release an isolation; only the AEP de-isolates through the controlled process
- Never remove another person’s lock or tag — this is a dismissible offence

### SLIDE 26 — Work at height
- **Fall protection is required from 1.8 m** (or where a fall hazard exists)
- Use approved scaffolding, platforms or MEWPs; harness with **double lanyard and shock absorber**, anchored to a certified point (> 22 kN), **100% tie-off**
- Scaffolding: only erected/modified by certified scaffolders, **green tag** valid, inspected before each shift/after alteration, no work on red-tagged scaffolds
- Ladders: short-duration work only, secured, 3 points of contact, no carrying loads, no working from the top steps
- Barricade the area below; tether tools; never throw materials; protect openings and edges

### SLIDE 27 — Lifting operations
- **All lifts require a permit**; critical lifts (load > 75% of capacity, multi-crane, over live plant/energised equipment, non-routine) require a detailed engineered lifting plan
- Requirements: certified crane and lifting tackle (third-party certificates), competent operator, rigger and banksman, exclusion zone, taglines
- Outriggers fully extended on timber pads on firm ground; verify ground bearing capacity
- **No lifting in wind above 32 km/h** or in poor visibility; never lift over personnel or live equipment
- Load control: no side pulls, no shock loading, nobody under the load

### SLIDE 28 — Excavation and trenching
- A permit is required for any ground penetration deeper than **30 cm**
- Obtain the signed **underground utility clearance** (electrical/cables/earthing grid) before digging — **hand-dig trial pits near services**
- Protective systems (shoring, benching or sloping) are mandatory for excavations deeper than **1.2 m**
- Spoil heaps set back at least **1.0 m** from the edge; barricade and sign all excavations; ladders for egress every 7.5 m
- Inspect after rain or any ground movement; keep plant and vehicles away from the edge; never enter an unprotected trench

### SLIDE 29 — Confined spaces
- Entry only with a **Confined Space Entry Permit**, trained entrants/attendants and a rescue plan
- Atmospheric testing before entry and continuously during entry: **O₂, LEL, H₂S, CO** by an Authorized Gas Tester
- Ventilation, isolation (LOTO) and rescue equipment in place; the attendant stays at the entry at all times
- **Never enter to rescue** without breathing apparatus and the rescue team — most confined-space fatalities are would-be rescuers
- Excavations are classified as confined spaces only by risk assessment, not automatically

### SLIDE 30 — Hot work and fire prevention
- **Hot Work Permit** required for welding, cutting, grinding, soldering, thermal spraying or any spark/flame/heat above 100 °C
- Requirements: competent certified welder, fire blanket containment, **10 m clearance** of combustibles, dedicated **Fire Watch during the work and for 30 minutes after**, minimum two extinguishers on site, gas cylinders secured upright, separated (fuel/O₂) and capped
- Notify the area and the fire point before starting; check for hidden combustibles; screens to protect others
- **Never** perform hot work where a flammable atmosphere may exist or near fuel/chemical areas without a gas test

### SLIDE 31 — Hand and power tools
- Use the right tool for the job; inspect before use; remove defective tools from service immediately
- Portable electrical tools: 230 V with **30 mA RCD**, armoured/undamaged cables, earthed (verify earthing measurement), no taped joints
- Angle grinders: guard in place, correct disc for the material, no disc over-speed, wear **full face shield (EN 166)** + safety glasses + gloves
- Secure the workpiece; avoid cutting towards your body; no loose clothing, no jewelry, no rings, tie back long hair
- Compressed air is not for cleaning clothes or skin

### SLIDE 32 — Manual handling and ergonomics
- Assess the load: weight, size, grip, centre of gravity, distance and destination
- Use mechanical aids or get help; bend knees, keep the load close, no twisting while lifting
- Plan breaks and vary tasks; report any pain early — do not wait for it to become an injury
- Team lifting with a leader and clear commands for heavy or awkward loads

### SLIDE 33 — PPE: mandatory minimum on site
- Helmet with **chin strap** + high-visibility vest
- **Flame-retardant coverall** (visibility class 1)
- Safety shoes with toe protection and puncture resistance (**S3/S1P**)
- Safety glasses (ANSI/EN certified)
- Protective gloves (task-specific) and hearing protection in high-noise areas (**> 85 dB(A)**)
- Additional PPE is specified by the task risk assessment: arc-rated PPE (Category 2/4), face shield, insulated gloves, full-body harness, chemical-resistant PPE, respiratory protection
- **Respiratory protection is mandatory where dust/vapour is identified (e.g., FFP3) and requires face-fit testing before use**
- PPE must be in good condition and a good fit — damaged PPE is replaced free of charge

### SLIDE 34 — Health, heat stress and hydration
- Heat stress is managed by **WBGT monitoring** (monitoring starts when ambient temperature exceeds **40 °C**) with published work/rest cycles per task intensity
- **Acclimatisation** applies to all new arrivals and to anyone returning from leave: reduced workload and increased rest in the first days
- Drink water regularly (**1.0–1.2 litres per hour** for light work in high heat); use oral rehydration salts; shaded, ventilated rest areas are provided
- **Lone working is prohibited** on high-risk tasks — use the buddy system
- Warning signs: headache, dizziness, cramps, nausea, confusion, hot dry skin, fainting → move to shade, cool the body, give fluids if conscious, call the medic/UHF Ch.1 immediately — **heat stroke is a medical emergency**
- Ramadan and summer peak-hour arrangements are published separately and must be followed

### SLIDE 35 — First aid and medical emergency
- First-aid points, trained first aiders, eye-wash stations, stretchers and the site clinic locations are marked on the plot plan
- Everyone must know: the nearest first-aid point, the site medic, the emergency number and the route to the nearest hospital

- Meet the ambulance at the main gate and escort it in; report the emergency to the EHS Control Room immediately
- Fitness for work: valid medical certificate, report any illness/injury/medication that affects your ability to work safely

### SLIDE 36 — Environment: our obligations on site

### SLIDE 37 — Waste management
- Segregate at source: general waste · recyclables (metal, cable, paper, plastic) · **hazardous waste** (oil-contaminated, batteries, filters, chemicals, e-waste) · construction/demolition waste
- Hazardous waste is stored in labelled, covered, bunded areas and moves only with **MOTAT records and a licensed transporter**
- Never burn waste, never mix hazardous with general waste, never dump into trenches, watercourses or empty land
- Material and waste removal requires a **Material Gate Pass**
- Keep all waste areas tidy, marked and away from drains

### SLIDE 38 — Chemicals and hazardous substances
- No chemical may be brought to site without a **Safety Data Sheet (SDS)** and registration in the electronic substance register
- Read the label and the SDS; use the specified PPE; never decant into unlabelled containers
- Store flammables separately in approved cabinets, in limited quantities; keep oxidisers away from fuels
- Eye-wash and emergency shower must be accessible where chemicals are handled
- Report all contact, inhalation or ingestion immediately

### SLIDE 39 — Fire classes and extinguishers (corrected)

### SLIDE 40 — Using a fire extinguisher (PASS)
- **P**ull the pin · **A**im at the base of the fire · **S**queeze the handle · **S**weep side to side
- Hold the extinguisher upright; stand between the fire and your escape route, back to the exit
- For CO₂: hold the **insulated horn**, never the metal parts (cold burn risk)
- **Only fight a small fire if you are trained and have a clear escape route** — otherwise raise the alarm and evacuate
- Call the alarm first; evacuate if the fire grows, produces dense smoke or involves gas cylinders
- Extinguishers are inspected monthly and serviced annually — never use a defective or missing extinguisher: report it
